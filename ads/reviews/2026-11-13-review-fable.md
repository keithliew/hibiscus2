# Review of the 5 hibiscus2 November Search ads (Fable, 13 Nov 2026)

Review only. No file other than this one was written, and Google Ads was not opened. Sources read: `approved-facts.md`, `ads/2026-11-ad-copy.json`, `ads/2026-11-campaign-plan.md` and the brief. I could not see the live ads, so anything about pins or settings inside Google Ads is marked "check in Google Ads".

Character counts: Latin characters count 1; Chinese characters and full-width punctuation count 2 (Google's rule). Every proposed line was counted by script and scanned against the banned-word list in `approved-facts.md` section 10; none is over the limit and none matches a banned term. `*` marks a line that differs from the current copy.

## The main points

1. **Ad 2 reads strangely because Google glues two separate descriptions into one paragraph, and ours were not written to be glued.** The second one has no subject ("Run day to day by…"), the price and the pool each appear twice, and nothing in the ad says the villas are for sale.
2. **Ad 2's "Average" rating is probably not about the wording**, because ad 3 has the same wording and is rated "Excellent". Something differs between the two live ads. That needs a two-minute look in Google Ads before any rewrite.
3. **Ad 1's "Poor" rating is caused by the two pins.** That is the price of the buyer filter and is worth paying. Ad Strength is Google's score for variety, not for sales. The fix is to give each pinned slot two or three buyer-filter lines, not to unpin.
4. **The numbers are from two days of serving** (the campaign started 11 Nov) and 52 clicks. "Conversions" are WhatsApp button clicks, and the one lead that was checked was a holiday-seeker. Nothing here proves any ad is winning yet.

## Ad 1: group B, existing-resort searches, buyers only (POOR, 22 clicks, 4 conversions)

### Diagnosis
- The pinned pair "Lexis Hibiscus 2: For Sale | Buying, Not Booking a Stay?" is the best piece of copy in the campaign. It does its job and should stay.
- Weak spot: the word **"Book"** in "Book a Private Viewing" (headline 3 position, and description 4). On a search for the hotel's name, "Book" is exactly the word a hotel guest is looking for. Use "Arrange".
- "Managed by Lexis Hotel Group", "Same Group, New Resort" and "15-Year Rental Programme" can land in position 3 and pull the ad back towards sounding like a hotel or a holiday rental. "Rental" on its own is ambiguous to a holiday-maker.
- No headline contains "Port Dickson" or "price", although the keywords are "lexis hibiscus port dickson for sale", "[lexis hibiscus port dickson]" and "lexis hibiscus price".
- Treat the 4 conversions with care. This group took the hotel-name traffic, and the first lead (probably from here) wanted a holiday stay. Four button clicks from people who may be hotel guests is not yet evidence that this is the best ad.

### Why Ad Strength is low
1. **Pinning (the main cause).** One headline is pinned to position 1 and one to position 2, so Google can only vary position 3. Google marks any ad like this down, usually to Poor, however good the other lines are.
2. Missing keyword words in the headlines ("Port Dickson", "price"). Minor.
3. Several unpinned headlines say nearly the same thing as each other. Minor.

**The fix that moves it most without losing the filter:** pin two lines to position 1 and three lines to position 2, all of them buyer-filter lines. Google gets variety; every version of the ad still says "for sale, not a stay". Expect Poor to become Average at best. Good or Excellent would need the pins removed, which I do not recommend.

### Risk
- **Trade-off, stated plainly:** the pins stay, so the rating will not reach Good. Do not accept Google's "unpin" recommendation on this ad.
- Editing a responsive search ad sends it back through review and resets its learning. On a 20-day run that costs a day or so.
- The minimum-change option, if you want to leave a converting ad alone: change only "Book" to "Arrange" (two lines) and add the three extra pinned lines.

## Ad 2: group A, project name, lands on the villas page (AVERAGE, 18 clicks, 1 conversion)

### Diagnosis: what makes it read strangely
What the owner saw:

> Lexis Hibiscus 2 Port Dickson | Villas From RM860,000 | Private Pool in Every Villa
> Own a 2-bedroom overwater villa with its own private pool. From RM860,000. Run day to day by Lexis Hotel Group under a 15-year rental programme. Ask for the terms.

1. **Two descriptions are glued together and the join is broken.** "From RM860,000. Run day to day by Lexis Hotel Group…" The second sentence has no subject. It was written as a follow-on to a sentence Google is free to separate it from. Read cold, "Run" looks like an instruction, and the reader has to work out what is being run.
2. **It says the same things twice.** The price is in headline 2 and again in the description. The private pool is in headline 3 and again in the description. Four lines, two facts repeated.
3. **Chopped fragments.** "From RM860,000." and "Ask for the terms." are not sentences. Four full stops in two lines gives it a telegram rhythm.
4. **"Ask for the terms" is cold and vague.** Terms of what? It sounds like small print, not an invitation.
5. **"Day to day" next to "rental" can be read as daily rental.** With the hotel's name in headline 1 and a pool in headline 3, a holiday-maker can read this as a villa you rent by the day. "Rental programme" is never explained as something for owners.
6. **Nothing says "for sale", and nothing asks the reader to do anything.** The three headlines are three labels (a name, a price, a feature). The only buyer word is "Own", in the middle of the description. There is no viewing, no WhatsApp, no agent in this combination. This breaks the campaign's own rule that every ad must make clear these are villas for sale.

### Why Ad Strength is low
- **The wording cannot be the main cause.** Ad 3 is in the same ad group, has the same copy and the same keywords, and is rated Excellent. Google scores Ad Strength from the headlines, descriptions, keywords and pins, not from the landing page. So ad 2 as it sits in Google Ads is not identical to ad 3.
- **Most likely causes, in order (check in Google Ads):** (a) one or more headlines pinned on ad 2, which would also explain why it always shows the same three headlines in the same order; (b) fewer headlines or descriptions on ad 2 than on ad 3, for example after the 11 Nov showroom edit; (c) a rating that has not refreshed since that edit.
- **The fix that moves it most:** open ad 2 and ad 3 side by side, compare pins and the count of headlines and descriptions, and make ad 2 match ad 3. That should lift it to Excellent with no rewrite. It also matters for the test: if the two ads differ, the landing-page test is not a clean test.
- Secondary, for both ads: headlines lack the words in the keywords "for sale", "price" and "villa" as a phrase next to the project name.

### Proposed rewrite (one shared version for ads 2 and 3; the test restarts)
No pins. Instead, every description is a complete sentence that makes sense on its own and in any pair, and each one carries a buyer word (for sale, owners, buying, prices). Price and pool appear once in the descriptions.

Every description covers it, but if you want certainty, pin "Lexis Hibiscus 2: For Sale" and "Villas for Sale From RM860,000" together to position 2. Cost: a lower rating on both ads.

## Things that apply to every ad
- **"Book" attracts hotel guests.** Use "Arrange" for viewings and consultations across the campaign, including the "Book a Viewing" sitelink.
- **"Rental programme" needs "owner" beside it** wherever it appears, including the "15-Year Rental Programme" callout and the "Rental Programme" sitelink.
- **Ad Strength is not a results score.** Ad 1 is Poor and has 4 of the 5 conversions; ads 3 and 4 are Excellent and have none. Do not let the rating drive decisions over the lead sheet.
- **Count leads, not button clicks.** Match each conversion to a WhatsApp Ref code before deciding which ad is working.

## Top 3 changes, in priority order (plain English)

1. **Open ad 2 and ad 3 side by side in Google Ads and make them truly identical.** They are supposed to be twins, yet Google rates one Average and one Excellent, which means something differs (most likely a pinned headline or a missing line on ad 2). This takes minutes, costs nothing, probably lifts ad 2's rating without any rewrite, and it is needed for your landing-page test to be a fair test.
2. **Fix the strange wording on ads 2 and 3 with the one shared version above, and accept that the test restarts.** The new descriptions are each a full sentence that makes sense alone, the price and pool are said once, and every line tells a holiday-maker this is a villa to buy. Change both ads at the same moment.
3. **On ad 1, keep the buyer filter and widen it; do not unpin.** Add the extra pinned lines (two choices for the first headline, three for the second) and change "Book" to "Arrange". The rating should move from Poor to Average. It will never be Excellent while it filters, and that is the right trade, because without the filter you pay for people who want a hotel room.

Next after these: find out why ad 5 never shows (the "hotel" block on its own keyword), remove or confirm "furnished", and apply the Chinese for-sale lines at the 16 Nov check.
