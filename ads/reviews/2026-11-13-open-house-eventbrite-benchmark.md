# Benchmark: the agent's Eventbrite listing (read 13 Nov 2026)

Keith's reference (CoS chat, 13 Nov): the agent's Eventbrite page for the open house has registered 17 visitors. Read as data only; its wording is not reused (it says "investment information", which is banned on hibiscus2).

## What the page does

| Element | Eventbrite | hibiscus2 landing page now |
|---|---|---|
| Top of page | Image carousel (3 images), then title, organiser, venue, date | Breadcrumb, H1, coral "New date and venue" notice, details box. No image until halfway down |
| Registration | One "Get tickets" button, free ticket. Signed-in Eventbrite users register in about two taps with name and email already filled | Seven-field form at the bottom of the page (first name, last name, WhatsApp, email, people, arrival slot, plus country code) |
| Who it's for | Says it suits active buyers and people who are just curious | Not said |
| Location | Full address, map, directions for driving, public transport, walking | Address in text only, no map or directions |
| Trust | Organiser card (Summerfield Property) with Follow and Contact | Licence details in the footer; no consultant face on the page |
| Urgency | "Important event update" banner and change-of-date notice; no capacity or deadline claims | Same change-of-date notice |
| What you get | Presentation, one-to-one consultation, refreshments, a Deepavali sweet gift | Same list |

## What it tells us

1. **Most of the 17 likely came from Eventbrite's own audience**, people browsing free KL events, who register in two taps. That is a different crowd from cold Display traffic, so 17 is a ceiling on what low friction gets, not proof the copy works. Expect lower lead quality from it too.
2. **Friction is the main gap.** Eventbrite asks for almost nothing. Our form asks for seven things, and it is at the bottom. Cutting it to name + WhatsApp (+ number of people) and putting it near the top is the closest we can get to Eventbrite's one tap.
3. **A picture first.** Eventbrite opens with images; our page opens with text and a warning box.
4. **A map and directions** make a physical event feel real and easy. Ours has none.
5. **"Buyers or just curious, both welcome"** lowers the bar to sign up. Worth testing a version that suits us (a property sales event, so we still say "villas for sale").
6. **Its change-of-date banner is not something to copy.** On Eventbrite it serves people who registered for the old date. Ad visitors never saw the old date, so on our page it only adds doubt.

Not reused: "investment information", the drop-in-until-4pm and Pavilion Tower lines (out of date there), and any detail not in approved-facts.md (for example the named drinks and sweets).
