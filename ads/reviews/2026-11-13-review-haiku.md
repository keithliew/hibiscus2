# Ad copy review: hibiscus2 November Search ads (13 Nov 2026)

Reviewer: Haiku 5.5, independent review. Review only. No existing file was edited and nothing in Google Ads was touched.

Read: `approved-facts.md`, `ads/2026-11-ad-copy.json`, `ads/2026-11-campaign-plan.md`, and the brief.

Character counts are the number of characters per line (headlines must be 30 or fewer, descriptions 90 or fewer). `*` marks a line that is new compared with the current copy in that ad. `[PIN 1]` and `[PIN 2]` mark pinned lines.

---

## The short version for the owner

- **Ad 2 reads strangely** because it is three fragments joined with bars, like a price tag, and the same "Villas From" is used for a price and for a size.
- **Ad Strength is low for ads 1 and 2 for different reasons.** Ad 1 has two pinned lines and most of its other lines never say "price" or "Lexis Hibiscus 2". Ad 2 repeats the same words across its lines and never says "for sale".
- **Most important:** a WhatsApp click is not a buyer. The 4 conversions on ad 1 are clicks, and the one lead we checked probably came through that same ad group.
- **Before anything changes:** the dates and landing pages in the brief do not match the campaign plan. Check those first (see "Checks before approving").

## Ad 1: Group B, existing-resort searches, buyers only (pins kept)

**1. Diagnosis**
- The ad does its main job: the two pinned lines say "For Sale" and "Buying, Not Booking a Stay?".
- The weak point is the rest of the ad. Several lines are generic: "Own a Villa Next Door", "Same Group, New Resort", "2-Bedroom Pool Villas". "Overwater Villas for Sale" uses "overwater", which is not confirmed for Lexis Hibiscus 2 in `approved-facts.md`. That word is only confirmed for the existing resort.
- The exact-match keyword **[lexis hibiscus port dickson]** is the existing hotel's own name. Most people typing it are hotel guests. The day 1 search-term report shows 9 clicks on it, costing RM21.30, and both conversions. The one holiday-stay lead probably came through this ad group (the plan says so, but it is not confirmed). Ad copy can only help if the person reads it, and a hotel guest typing the hotel's name will often click past "Buying, Not Booking a Stay?".

**2. Why Ad Strength is low**
- Two pinned lines leave Google only 13 lines to mix. That limits how many different combinations it can show, which lowers the score.
- Keyword match is thin. The searches in this group are "price", "for sale", "property" and the hotel name. Only one headline uses "Lexis Hibiscus", and none says "price" (only "Get Villa Prices", which is plural).
- The word "villa" appears in about 8 of the 15 headlines, so many lines look alike to Google.
- Google does not publish the exact scoring. These are the causes that match what the copy shows.
- **The fix that moves the score most:** more lines that match the searches ("for sale", "price", "2-Bedroom") and fewer repeated "villa" lines. Removing the pins would lift the score more, but it removes the buyer filter (see the trade-off below).

## Ad 2: Group A, project name, /villas/port-dickson (test copy, shared with ad 3)

**1. Diagnosis: what reads strangely**
- The three lines the owner sees are joined with bars: "Lexis Hibiscus 2 Port Dickson | Villas From RM860,000 | Private Pool in Every Villa". Together they read like a shop sign or a price list, not a sentence someone would say to a buyer.
- **"Villas From 858 sq ft"** (headline 14) uses "From", which only makes sense with a price, for a size. A reader can take it as a price, and the same ad already shows "Villas From RM860,000". It is the strangest line in the ad.
- The ad never says **"for sale"** in any headline. It says "Own a villa" only in one description. For an ad that has to filter out hotel guests, that is a gap.
- "Overwater Villas, Port Dickson" (headline 7) uses "overwater", which is not confirmed for Lexis Hibiscus 2 in `approved-facts.md`.
- "Next to Lexis Hibiscus Resort" (headline 11) puts the existing hotel's name in front of people who may want a hotel room.
- Description 2 ends "Ask for the terms." A buyer does not know which terms, so the line is vague.

**2. Why Ad Strength is average**
- Repeats: "Villas From" appears twice (headlines 2 and 14), "WhatsApp" twice, "Private Pool" twice, and "Viewing" twice.
- It does not use the searched words "for sale" or "2.0" anywhere.
- Lines 7, 11 and 14 are the weakest and add repetition or unclear meaning.
- The fix that moves the score most: replace the repeated and unclear lines with unique lines that use the searched words ("For Sale", "Prices", "2-Bedroom").

**3. One proposed rewrite (shared by ads 2 and 3)**

Because ads 2 and 3 are a landing-page test, both must carry this identical version. This **restarts the test**. The test has only 28 clicks and 1 conversion so far, so nothing is being lost by restarting it.

The brief's landing pages (/villas/for-sale, /villas/port-dickson, /programme/port-dickson) also differ from the plan's (/villas/ and /rental-programme/). 4. **Conversions are clicks, not buyers.** Use the lead sheet to judge each ad.
